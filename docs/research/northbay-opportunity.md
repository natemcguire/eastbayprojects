## Observed customer journey and the question to test

The visible foodservice journey is: discover a culinary product, submit the wholesale form, receive distributor information, then buy through a distributor. The website does not expose what happens inside the sales team or distributor after submission. We have not measured response times, conversion, existing follow-up, or order visibility.

**Hypothesis:** a small shared process that follows a qualified local café from inquiry through its first and repeat order could improve the return on demand Straus already generates. First establish whether this process is already effective. A website form and a distributor handoff are normal channel operations, not evidence that leads are being lost.

## Recommended first pilot: more repeat café accounts in the North Bay

**Scope:** one product family, Barista Milk; one participating distributor territory serving Sonoma or Marin; one agreed cohort of qualified café prospects. Agree geography, sample size, and timing after inspecting demand volume and distributor coverage. A six-week implementation experiment is a planning assumption, not a delivery commitment; continue measurement long enough to observe repeat ordering.

1. **Map the handoff.** The Operations Expert follows several real inquiries with the sales team and distributor, identifies each owner and required decision, and establishes the current baseline. Confirm availability, case sizes, delivery coverage, commercial terms, and whether an account is genuinely new.
2. **Connect the smallest useful data set.** The FDE links approved inquiry records, distributor acceptance, and first/repeat-order evidence using supported integrations or agreed exports. Reuse the current CRM and distributor processes where possible. API access, permitted data sharing, and order-level visibility are discovery dependencies, not assumed capabilities.
3. **Run a bounded sales workflow.** Give the team a view of prospects needing qualification, distributor acknowledgment, product evaluation, or follow-up. Establish an owner and next action for each handoff. Any automated customer communication follows the business's approved process.
4. **Measure account activation and economics.** Review the cohort weekly, including reasons accounts do not progress. Compare with a comparable earlier or concurrent cohort; account for seasonal demand, distributor coverage, and existing accounts. Expand only if the economics and team adoption warrant it.

**Primary measures:** qualified inquiry-to-first-order rate; time to first order; repeat-order rate over a window agreed around actual purchasing cadence; incremental cases sold; contribution after product, distributor, promotion, and servicing costs. Supporting measures: time awaiting handoff, manual follow-up minutes per account, and staff use of the workflow. A referral accepted by a distributor is an intermediate measure, not revenue.

**Discovery questions:**

- How many North Bay café inquiries are qualified each month, and what happens to them today?
- Can the sales team confirm first and repeat purchases through each distributor? If not, what evidence can the distributor provide?
- Which product/distributor combinations have capacity and margin to support additional accounts?
- Who owns follow-up, and what prevents an interested café from ordering: availability, pricing, delivery, evaluation, or something else?
- Is the useful intervention a configuration change, a shared operating procedure, a small integration, or no change at all?

## Why the two-person model fits

**Operations Expert:** owns process discovery, the economic baseline, distributor relationships, handoff rules, staff adoption, and the decision about whether the experiment merits expansion. This requires operational and commercial judgment, not only project coordination.

**Forward Deployed Engineer:** works beside the team, traces the data, configures existing tools, implements and maintains any small integrations or interface needed, and verifies that measurements are reliable. The engineer is accountable for a workflow people can run, rather than a demonstration detached from their systems.

Together they connect customer demand to fulfilled, repeat business. For East Bay Projects, this is a clearer offer than “AI automation”: a defined operating result, with software and hands-on implementation selected around that result. Paid ads or creator partnerships become a later test once the team can measure activation and supply the resulting demand.

## Short prospective case-study draft

### Straus Family Creamery: from café interest to repeat wholesale orders

*Opportunity study · North Bay, California · Based on public research. Straus is not an East Bay Projects client; this work has not been implemented.*

Straus makes organic dairy products in Sonoma County and offers a dedicated Barista Milk range for culinary businesses. Interested cafés submit a wholesale inquiry and are connected with distributors. [Manufacturing](https://www.strausfamilycreamery.com/sustainable-manufacturing/), [Barista Milk](https://www.strausfamilycreamery.com/culinary-professionals/barista-milk/).

We would investigate one question: could a clearer path from inquiry to first and repeat order help grow foodservice sales in the North Bay?

An Operations Expert would work with sales and a distributor to map the handoff and establish a baseline. A Forward Deployed Engineer would connect the minimum necessary data and tools so the team can see the next action for each account.

The first pilot would cover one product family and one distributor territory. Success would be measured in repeat purchasing and contribution after costs, alongside the time required to manage each account. Those are proposed measures, not achieved results.
