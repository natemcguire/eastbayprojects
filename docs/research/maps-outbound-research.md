# Research: tailored proposals for local businesses

Research date: 2026-09-11. Prepared for the East Bay Projects business plan. This is public-source research, not a prospect contact database or evidence of client work. No messages, calls, forms, purchases, account creation, or outreach were performed.

## What the linked post actually establishes

The [exact Tristen Palori post](https://x.com/tristenpalori/status/2098456523072537015) was read in the signed-in browser. Public web retrieval returned 403; the browser loaded the complete text and two attached screenshots. This is a text-and-images post, not a video.

Palori reports receiving an unsolicited text linking to a proposed replacement website with three additional versions. He says a follow-up text arrived immediately after he opened it, and describes the service as $99/month. His suggested 1,000 prospects per day, 0.1% conversion, and roughly $36,000 MRR after a year are hypothetical arithmetic. The post does not document the sender's actual volume, conversion, revenue, churn, operating costs, or automation architecture. He also expresses doubt about service quality.

The screenshots show Gleaming Studios as the preview domain, a time-limited activation banner, and an initial text followed by a message referencing the visit. We did not follow the individualized preview or intake URLs.

[Gleaming Studios' public homepage](https://gleamingstudios.com/) independently advertises $99.95/month, no setup charge, a five-page site, hosting and SSL, Google Business Profile optimization, monthly SEO updates, and a social landing page. It says previews arrive within 48 hours, offers two revision rounds, and describes a cancel-anytime monthly subscription. These are the vendor's advertised terms, not independently verified delivery or performance.

**Useful mechanism:** a specific, useful artifact can make an unsolicited introduction easier to evaluate. **Unproven business claim:** that mass texting and cheap website subscriptions produce an automated, profitable business.

## Recommended East Bay adaptation

Sell one business result that a small engineer-and-operations team can implement and measure. The proposal opens a conversation about that result. A website refresh can be part of delivery, but should follow the actual bottleneck.

For an initial field-service vertical, use this offer hypothesis: **help an established local operator turn suitable inquiries into scheduled, profitable work with fewer administrative handoffs.**

- Operations Expert: observe how work enters the business, qualify the economic problem, define handoffs and exceptions, obtain staff adoption, and own the weekly operating review.
- Forward Deployed Engineer: connect the existing forms, inbox, CRM, estimating, and scheduling tools; build missing interfaces; instrument the workflow; maintain the deployed system.
- Shared ownership: demonstrate the change on a bounded workflow, measure performance against a baseline, and expand only when evidence supports it.

The durable assets are the repeatable workflow, field knowledge, integrations, and credible before/after evidence. The prospecting system serves that business; it is not the whole business.

## Selection rules for an initial pilot

These are proposed screening criteria, not measured market facts.

1. Choose one local service area and one workflow for the first campaign. Alexandria/Northern Virginia landscaping is a coherent starting point given the existing Kingstowne research. North Bay landscaping provides a second geography for later replication.
2. Prefer established employers with recurring work plus larger projects, identifiable service boundaries, multiple staff roles, and enough job volume to measure a change.
3. Require an observable commercial context: estimate requests, service packages, maintenance contracts, multiple crews or service lines. A dated website alone is weak evidence of a valuable operational problem.
4. Qualify capacity and economics in the call. More inquiries can be harmful when the business cannot service them; faster estimates matter only for work the business wants.
5. Confirm decision ownership, existing systems, budget, and staff access. Public pages cannot tell us gross margins, response time, route efficiency, unused capacity, or willingness to buy.
6. Exclude businesses seeking only the lowest-price website, businesses without capacity for implementation, and corporate subsidiaries whose local team lacks purchasing authority.

## Discovery and research workflow

Google Maps can be a manual discovery surface. We inspected the visible results for [Alexandria landscaping](https://www.google.com/maps/search/landscaping+Alexandria+VA/) and [Sonoma/Marin commercial landscaping](https://www.google.com/maps/search/commercial+landscaping+Sonoma+Marin+California/), then verified relevant facts on business-owned websites. We did not scrape, export, or preserve a bulk Maps dataset, review archive, or contact list.

The operating workflow should be:

1. Researcher manually identifies a small number of plausible businesses in a defined territory.
2. Research agent reads their first-party service, about, process, and contact pages; records dated source links and facts separately from hypotheses.
3. Operations Expert rejects weak fits and selects one plausible workflow and one economic question per business.
4. Engineer creates a small reusable demonstration with synthetic data only after that review. Do not build an entire unsolicited application.
5. Produce a concise proposal artifact; human review checks factual accuracy, scope, claims and presentation.
6. Use the approved outreach channel and recipient; the parent business plan covers consent, suppression and communications requirements. Track actual replies and attended calls separately from page opens.
7. On a call, replace assumptions with the operator's numbers and workflow. Only then commit implementation scope or expected economics.

Google's [Maps Platform terms, section 3.2.3](https://cloud.google.com/maps-platform/terms) prohibit exporting/scraping Maps content outside the services and explicitly include copying/saving business names, addresses and reviews. Its [Places API policy](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/web-service/policies) generally restricts caching/storage, with a place-ID exception. Paying for an API or a third-party scraper does not by itself authorize a permanent prospect database. The [consumer Maps terms](https://www.google.com/help/terms_maps/) also restrict copying and bulk downloading. Build durable research from independently verified first-party sources or appropriately licensed datasets; do not make bulk Maps extraction a dependency.

## The proposal that should earn a call

Suggested format: one polished, mobile-friendly page, with an optional two-minute narrated walkthrough.

- A business-specific title naming the workflow, such as **From completed landscape project to recurring maintenance**.
- Two or three dated, sourced observations, followed by the specific question they raise.
- A simple before/proposed-after workflow. Label the existing workflow as unknown unless the business has actually described it.
- One working example using synthetic records, visibly marked as a concept.
- A four-week pilot outline: establish baseline; implement one handoff; train a small team; compare results and decide whether to expand.
- A short list of required inputs and exclusions, plus a success metric the owner can agree or revise.
- A clear invitation to a short qualification call, with the engineer and Operations Expert named.

Use a plain commercial introduction that leads with the useful observation. Avoid a claim that the company is losing a particular dollar amount, pretending the concept is authorized work, fabricated scarcity, or a follow-up that tells the recipient they are being watched. A page view can reflect a security scanner and is weaker evidence than an actual response.

The first campaign should test whether this artifact gets the right operator into a substantive conversation and whether the resulting pilot can be delivered profitably. It should not assume the conversion rate or autopilot economics in the X post.
