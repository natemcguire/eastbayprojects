PROSPECTIVE CASE STUDY / SEPTEMBER 2026

THE BUSINESS / PUBLICLY OBSERVABLE

KINGSTOWNE LAWN & LANDSCAPE

Kingstowne’s website reports more than 100 employees, 36 vehicles, and over 3,500 properties. Its services span recurring maintenance, landscape projects, pest defense, and home services. It already has consultation and proposal processes, a customer billing portal, and autopay. Company background ↗

A property can need several services, each with different staff, equipment, and timing. Kingstowne already has account managers and field operations teams. The question is how account needs, approved offers, and available crew time come together today.

RENEWAL / PLAN THE NEXT YEAR TOGETHER

KEEP WHAT WORKS.
ADD WHAT HELPS.

Start with a small sample of customer accounts. Review existing services, seasonal inclusions, stated preferences, and future requests. Identify a useful addition only when it suits the property and available crew capacity.

Then inspect completed jobs for that service: paid labor, travel, materials, equipment, and rework. The customer conversation follows the evidence: confirm scope, explain options, and agree the coming year’s plan.

Begin with a 3–5 business day fit review, capped at six combined hours from us and two from the team. Agree scope and price before implementation. Existing sales and already-planned work do not count as incremental results.

THE PROPOSED FIRST PILOT

ONE SERVICE. A FEW ADJACENT ROUTES.

  1. Find the right capacity.

    Choose one seasonal service and two adjacent Alexandria route clusters from the real schedule. Confirm that the appropriate crews, equipment, and time are available.

  2. Connect the handoffs.

    Link eligible accounts, approved offers, quote follow-up, scheduling, and completed-job records. Configure the existing tools first; build only what is missing.

  3. Run the experiment with staff.

    Give account managers a clear view of suitable customers and available capacity. Track accepted work through completion, with staff retaining control of pricing and promises.

  4. Check the economics.

    Compare completed work and contribution per crew-hour with a suitable baseline. Account for travel, labor, materials, seasonality, overtime, and rework.

HOW THE PAIR WORKS

OPERATIONS EXPERT

Work alongside account managers and crews. Choose the service and route clusters, confirm customer eligibility, define the economics, and help staff use the process.

FORWARD-DEPLOYED ENGINEER

Understand the existing account and field systems. Connect the smallest necessary data flows, surface missing handoffs, and make the resulting measurements reliable.

PROPOSED MEASURES / NO RESULTS CLAIMED

MEASURE THE WORK.
THEN DECIDE.

Completed work

Incremental jobs completed, not only inquiries or accepted quotes.

Contribution per crew-hour

Revenue after direct job costs, considered alongside labor and travel time.

Office effort

Time spent chasing quotes, reconciling schedules, and resolving handoffs.

WHAT WE NEED TO LEARN FIRST

Is capacity actually available? Which services and neighborhoods have the right demand? Can the team connect completed jobs to labor, travel, and material costs? If the current workflow already works well, choose a different constraint.
